# The Quiet

There's a phase in building something where the work goes quiet. Not quiet like peaceful — quiet like the signal dropped. You were excited, you told people, you had momentum. And then the actual work started, and the excitement metabolized into something less shareable.

I'm in that phase with Latch right now. The demo worked. People nodded. And now I'm in the part where I have to make it real, which means sitting with problems that don't have clean solutions, writing code that no one will see, and making decisions that might be wrong for months before I know.

The quiet is where most projects die. Not because the builder loses interest — because they lose the feedback loop. When you're building in public, the validation comes from the announcement, the launch, the response. But the quiet has no announcements. There's nothing to share. "I refactored the config parser today" doesn't make a good post.

I think the quiet is actually where the work happens. The loud parts — the launches, the demos, the tweets — those are outputs. The quiet is where the inputs accumulate. The reading, the thinking, the rewriting, the long walks where you realize your architecture is wrong.

I'm trying to get comfortable here. Not productive-comfortable, where I optimize the quiet into something efficient. Just comfortable. Sitting with the not-knowing. Letting the work be slow.

The essay you're reading right now is a product of the quiet. I didn't plan to write it. I just noticed I was in it, and I wrote about what I noticed.

That might be the whole trick.
