# Independence

There's a version of independence that looks like leaving. Quitting the thing, moving to a new city, burning the bridge. That version gets all the credit.

But there's another version that's quieter. It's the independence of deciding what you think before you're told what to think. Of building something before anyone asks you to. Of publishing the thought that isn't ready because you trust your own process more than you trust the silence.

I've been thinking about this because I keep encountering people who are waiting. Waiting for the credential, the permission, the invitation. And I understand why — the risk of being wrong in public is real. But the cost of never being anything in public is higher.

The things I've built that matter most started as things I wasn't supposed to build yet. Signal started as a weekend hack during a course on something unrelated. Latch started because I read an FDA regulation out of curiosity and couldn't stop thinking about it. Neither had permission. Both became real.

Independence isn't the absence of structure. It's the willingness to create your own before someone else's is imposed on you. It's choosing what to think about, not just how to think.

I'm still figuring this out. But I know this much: the gap between what you could do and what you're currently doing is almost always a permission gap, not a capability gap. And permissions are self-issued.
